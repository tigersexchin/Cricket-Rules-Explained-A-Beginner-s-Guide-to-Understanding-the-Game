Cricket Rules Explained: A Beginner's Guide to Understanding the Game

Cricket is one of the most followed sports in the world, especially in India. Millions of fans enjoy watching international matches, domestic tournaments and local cricket competitions. However, for someone who is new to the sport, understanding all the rules can sometimes feel confusing.

From scoring runs to taking wickets, cricket has several basic rules that decide how a match is played. Once you understand these rules, following a cricket match becomes much easier and more enjoyable.

Whether you are watching your first international match or simply want to improve your cricket knowledge, this beginner-friendly guide explains the important rules of cricket in simple language.

1. Understanding the Basic Structure of Cricket

Cricket is played between two teams, with 11 players on each side.

One team bats while the other team bowls and fields. The batting team tries to score as many runs as possible, while the bowling team attempts to restrict the runs and take wickets.

After the first innings or scheduled batting period is completed, the teams usually switch roles.

The main objective is simple: score more runs than the opposing team while following the rules of the match format.

2. How Runs Are Scored in Cricket

Runs are the points scored by the batting team. There are several ways to add runs to the scoreboard.

Running Between the Wickets

When a batter hits the ball, both batters can run between the two sets of wickets.

Every completed run adds one run to the team's total.

Boundary Four

If the ball reaches the boundary after touching the ground inside the playing area, the batting team receives four runs.

Boundary Six

If the batter hits the ball over the boundary without it touching the ground inside the field, the team receives six runs.

Extra Runs

The batting team can also receive additional runs through certain bowling or fielding mistakes.

Common extras include:

  • Wides.
  • No-balls.
  • Byes.
  • Leg-byes.

These runs are added to the team's total even though they may not be credited to the individual batter.

3. What Is an Over in Cricket?

An over is a set of six legal deliveries bowled by one bowler from one end of the pitch.

After completing an over, another bowler delivers the next over from the opposite end.

For example, if a team completes 10 overs, it has faced 60 legal deliveries.

The number of overs available depends on the match format:

  • Test cricket: No fixed limit on the number of overs per innings.
  • One Day International: 50 overs per team in a standard match.
  • T20 cricket: 20 overs per team in a standard match.

Understanding overs is important because they determine how much time and how many deliveries a team has to score runs.

4. How Does a Team Lose Wickets?

A wicket represents the dismissal of a batter. In a standard innings, a team can lose up to 10 wickets.

There are several ways a batter can get out.

Bowled

A batter is bowled when a legal delivery hits the stumps and dislodges at least one bail.

Caught

A batter is caught when a fielder catches the ball directly after it has been hit by the batter, without the ball touching the ground.

Run Out

A batter can be run out when they are outside their crease and the fielding team legally breaks the relevant wicket before the batter makes their ground.

LBW (Leg Before Wicket)

A batter may be given out LBW when the ball hits their body instead of the bat and the conditions of the LBW rule are satisfied.

Stumped

A batter can be stumped when they are outside their crease and the wicketkeeper legally removes the bails while the batter is not attempting a run.

These are some of the most common dismissal methods in cricket.

5. Understanding the Roles of Cricket Players

Every player has a role that contributes to the team's performance.

Batter

Batters focus on scoring runs, building partnerships and helping their team reach a competitive total.

Bowler

Bowlers attempt to dismiss batters and control the number of runs scored by the opposition.

Wicketkeeper

The wicketkeeper stands behind the stumps and plays an important role in catching the ball, completing stumpings and supporting the fielding team.

All-Rounder

An all-rounder contributes with both batting and bowling.

Captain

The captain leads the team, makes strategic decisions and helps organise field placements and bowling changes.

6. What Are the Different Formats of Cricket?

Cricket is played in different formats, each with its own rules and match duration.

Test Cricket

Test cricket is the traditional international format. A match can last up to five days, and each team normally has two innings.

One Day International

An ODI match gives each team 50 overs in a standard full-length match. Teams need to balance careful batting with scoring opportunities.

T20 Cricket

T20 cricket is a shorter format in which each team receives 20 overs. It is known for quick matches and fast-paced gameplay.

7. What Are Powerplay Rules in Cricket?

Powerplay refers to specific overs during limited-overs cricket when fielding restrictions apply.

These restrictions limit the number of fielders allowed outside the 30-yard circle.

In standard T20 international cricket:

  • Overs 1–6 form the first powerplay.
  • A maximum of two fielders can be outside the circle during this period.
  • Different fielding restrictions apply during the remaining overs.

In ODI cricket, powerplay restrictions follow a different structure.

Powerplays encourage teams to balance attacking batting with strategic field placements.

8. What Happens When a Cricket Match Ends in a Tie?

A tied match occurs when both teams finish with exactly the same score under the applicable match conditions.

In many limited-overs competitions, a Super Over may be used to determine the winner.

During a Super Over, each team receives one additional over to score runs. The team scoring more runs wins, subject to the competition's playing conditions.

Some tournaments may have additional rules if the Super Over also ends in a tie.

9. Why Is Cricket Umpiring Important?

Umpires are responsible for ensuring that a cricket match follows the official playing rules.

They make decisions about dismissals, boundaries, wides, no-balls and other match situations.

In professional cricket, a third umpire may also assist with decisions using video replays and available technology.

The Decision Review System (DRS) allows teams to challenge certain on-field decisions according to the competition's rules.

Umpiring helps maintain fairness and ensures that the match is played according to established standards.

Frequently Asked Questions About Cricket Rules

How many players are there in a cricket team?

A standard cricket team has 11 players on the field, including the captain and wicketkeeper.

How many balls are there in one over?

One over consists of six legal deliveries. Wides and no-balls do not count towards the six legal deliveries.

How many runs are awarded for a six?

A batter receives six runs when the ball is hit over the boundary without first touching the ground inside the field.

What is the difference between a four and a six?

A four is awarded when the ball reaches the boundary after touching the ground inside the field. A six is awarded when the ball crosses the boundary without touching the ground inside the field.

What does LBW mean in cricket?

LBW stands for Leg Before Wicket. It is a dismissal method in which a batter can be given out when the ball hits their body instead of the bat and specific conditions of the rule are met.

Can a cricket match end in a tie?

Yes. A match can end in a tie when both teams finish with the same score. Depending on the competition, a Super Over or another tie-breaking procedure may be used.

Which cricket format has the shortest standard match duration?

T20 cricket is the shortest of the three main international formats, with each team receiving 20 overs.
